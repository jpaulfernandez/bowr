# bowr product context

Source: [bowr PRD](<bowr — PRD.md>). Design decisions: [UI, UX and information architecture](DESIGN.md). Updated 25 September 2026.

This is a pre-implementation design brief derived from the PRD and the user's instruction to make the remaining design decisions autonomously. Recommendations are not claims about an existing interface or validated user research.

## Register

product

## Users

The owner and 2–10 invited friends, each with a private wardrobe of roughly 100–300 pieces. Most use a phone browser; a laptop supports batch organization. A typical morning involves choosing clothes near a wardrobe in daylight, with little time and one hand holding the phone. Longer cataloging sessions happen at home. Users need to remember what they own, combine it confidently, and repeat outfits they liked.

## Product Purpose

Turn photos of owned clothing into a searchable Bower, help assemble outfits from real owned pieces, and record what was worn. The MVP is Gather, automatic tagging, Arrange, and Strut. Later phases add insights, purchase decisions, inspiration, optional fit guidance, native apps, and private sharing. The shared AI budget stays within the PRD's US$10 monthly ceiling; manual wardrobe use must survive an AI pause.

## Brand Personality

Observant, resourceful, warm. bowr notices the potential in what a person already owns. Bowerbird behavior inspires gathering, careful arrangement, personal preferences, and repeated use. It does not prescribe gender, attractiveness, or competitive display.

The wordmark is lowercase. Feature names follow the PRD; ordinary objects remain pieces, outfits, and photos. Pair unfamiliar feature names with plain explanations. Keep operational messages literal and brief.

## Anti-references

Avoid shopping-led homepages, public social feeds, outfit leaderboards, streak pressure, a chat box as the whole product, and a feature dashboard with ten equally weighted buttons. Avoid decorative bird interfaces, nest-shaped controls, feather textures, gradient text, and saturated backgrounds behind clothing. Competitor screens inform specific interaction patterns, not a copied visual identity.

## Design Principles

1. Make a small wardrobe useful immediately. Never require complete cataloging or a body profile before offering value.
2. Keep the user's pieces central. Suggestions resolve to owned item records; gaps remain visibly separate.
3. Make correction easy. User edits override inference, and recognition never silently creates wear history.
4. Preserve agency and privacy. Body information and photo retention are optional; sharing is explicit and later.
5. Reward reuse through useful memory. Wear history and Encore help people return to clothes they enjoy without guilt or scoring their bodies.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA on web, with generous touch targets, keyboard and screen-reader access, visible focus, text alternatives, and reduced motion. Selection and status never depend on color alone. Use inclusive garment categories, optional measurements, editable preferences, and descriptive fit language. Every swipe, drag, camera, and visual matching task needs an alternative.

## Visual Direction

Light only. A restrained Satin blue-violet identity with Flame orange and Gold accents on a pale cool canvas. Garment imagery receives most visual attention. Ship one identity at launch; defer optional interface accent selection. The PRD's Themes remain saved style briefs, separate from interface appearance.
